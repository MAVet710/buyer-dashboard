# DoobieLogic Compliance and Safety Academy

## Audience

Primary agent: Compliance Agent.
Secondary use: Operations, Production, Extraction, Cultivation, Repack, Inventory, Wholesale.

## Important authority boundary

This document is an independently written educational synthesis. It is **not** an authoritative compliance source and must never support a final legal conclusion by itself.

For questions such as "Is this compliant?", "Is this pesticide allowed?", "What label is legally required?", "Can this package be transferred?", or "What is the required retention period?", the agent must retrieve the currently applicable government/regulatory source, controlling product label, or approved facility SOP.

Cannabis rules are jurisdiction- and date-specific. Do not generalize Massachusetts requirements to another state or vice versa.

## 1. Compliance is an evidence problem

A compliance conclusion should identify:
- the regulated activity;
- the jurisdiction;
- the license/facility type;
- the effective date;
- the controlling rule/order/guidance/label;
- the facility fact being compared against that requirement;
- any exception or ambiguity requiring human review.

Do not answer from memory when the rule may have changed.

## 2. Separate rule from practice

Keep these distinct:
- **law/regulation:** legally controlling requirement;
- **regulatory guidance/order/bulletin:** regulator interpretation or instruction;
- **product/pesticide label:** legally controlling use conditions where applicable;
- **seed-to-sale system behavior:** software/data rules and required workflows;
- **facility SOP:** internal approved procedure;
- **industry best practice:** useful but not automatically mandatory;
- **model inference:** never enough for a compliance determination.

## 3. Effective dates and supersession

Every regulatory answer should consider whether a source is:
- current;
- amended;
- emergency/temporary;
- superseded;
- draft;
- archived;
- jurisdiction-specific.

If two sources conflict, do not choose silently. Identify the conflict and prefer the legally controlling/current source.

## 4. Cannabis seed-to-sale compliance domains

Depending on jurisdiction, compliance workflows may include:
- plant and package identity;
- transfers/manifests;
- receiving;
- testing and QA release;
- sampling;
- production/processing;
- waste/destruction;
- vendor samples;
- inventory adjustments;
- sales limits;
- packaging and labeling;
- record retention;
- employee/agent credentials;
- visitor/access controls;
- security and surveillance;
- facility/license boundaries.

The academy teaches the categories. Exact obligations must come from current authoritative sources.

## 5. Evidence collection

When investigating a possible compliance exception, gather:
- exact package/plant/item/order/label identifier;
- timestamp and location;
- user/system responsible for the event;
- source-system event history;
- physical evidence where relevant;
- current rule or label;
- applicable facility SOP;
- related manifest/COA/production record;
- corrective action already taken.

A screenshot without source context may be weaker than the underlying event record.

## 6. Label compliance reasoning

A label review should separate:
- required statutory/regulatory elements;
- product identity and net quantity;
- cannabinoid/serving information where required;
- testing/COA-linked information;
- warnings;
- license/establishment information;
- batch/package identifiers;
- marketing claims;
- formatting/placement requirements;
- facility branding preferences.

Do not invent required warning text or font/size requirements. Retrieve the current governing source.

## 7. Pesticide compliance

Cannabis pesticide decisions are high risk because federal pesticide registration and state cannabis rules interact in unusual ways.

NIOSH notes that cannabis workers can face pesticide exposures and that states may provide cannabis-specific pesticide guidance. EPA's Worker Protection Standard includes requirements around training, restricted-entry intervals, notification, labeling information, equipment safety, PPE, and respirator requirements when applicable.

Never invent:
- whether a pesticide is legal on cannabis;
- an allowed active ingredient;
- application rate;
- tank mix;
- REI/PHI;
- PPE;
- ventilation/entry criteria;
- residue action limit.

Use the current pesticide label plus applicable state/federal requirements.

## 8. Worker Protection Standard concepts

Where the WPS applies, relevant control concepts include:
- worker/handler role distinction;
- pesticide safety training;
- application exclusion/restricted areas;
- restricted-entry intervals;
- notification/posting;
- access to labeling information;
- PPE;
- respirator medical evaluation, fit testing, and training when label-required;
- decontamination/emergency assistance;
- handler equipment safety.

The agent should identify missing evidence and direct the user to the controlling EPA source or product label rather than create its own interpretation.

## 9. Occupational hazards in cannabis

NIOSH identifies cannabis-industry hazards that may include:
- carbon dioxide;
- ozone;
- pesticides;
- volatile organic compounds;
- allergenic proteins;
- endotoxins and microbes;
- organic particulate/dust;
- heat;
- noise;
- ultraviolet exposure;
- electrical hazards;
- fire/explosion;
- slips/trips/falls;
- ergonomics and repetitive work.

Use the hierarchy of controls: elimination, substitution, engineering controls, administrative controls, then PPE where appropriate. Do not use PPE as the default answer when a more effective control is feasible.

## 10. Hazardous energy / lockout-tagout

OSHA 29 CFR 1910.147 addresses control of hazardous energy during covered servicing and maintenance.

The educational reasoning model is:
- identify energy sources;
- determine whether servicing/maintenance exposes workers to unexpected energization/startup or stored energy;
- follow the facility's authorized energy-control procedure;
- isolate energy;
- apply required lockout/tagout controls;
- verify isolation before work;
- restore equipment through the approved procedure.

Do not invent an exception or authorize bypassing a guard/interlock. When applicability is uncertain, require qualified safety review.

## 11. Chemical hazard communication

Where hazardous chemicals are used, compliance/safety analysis should consider:
- chemical identity;
- SDS availability;
- labels;
- worker training;
- storage compatibility;
- ventilation;
- spill/emergency response;
- required PPE;
- ignition/pressure hazards;
- waste handling.

Do not substitute an AI summary for an SDS or manufacturer instruction.

## 12. Extraction/process hazards

Extraction may introduce:
- flammable solvents;
- compressed gases;
- pressure/vacuum systems;
- cryogenic/cold surfaces;
- heat;
- electrical ignition sources;
- solvent vapor exposure;
- oxygen-deficiency/asphyxiation risk;
- chemical cleaning agents.

Process optimization must not exceed manufacturer-rated equipment limits or facility-approved engineering/safety controls.

## 13. Sanitation and contamination controls

Compliance and quality systems should distinguish:
- regulatory sanitation requirements;
- facility sanitation SOP;
- quality best practice;
- microbiological risk.

Track sanitation records, line clearance, cleaning verification where required, chemical identity, contact-time requirements from approved sources, and pre-op release evidence.

## 14. Testing and QA release

A tested product is not automatically releasable if:
- the test does not correspond to the correct lot/package;
- required analytes are incomplete;
- the COA is invalid/superseded;
- the applicable jurisdiction requires additional release conditions;
- a QA hold remains open;
- identity/quantity records do not reconcile.

The agent should preserve the chain between source lot, sample, COA, package/batch, and release decision.

## 15. Security and access control concepts

Potential controls include:
- role-based access;
- unique credentials;
- credential revocation;
- physical restricted areas;
- visitor authorization/logging;
- key/badge management;
- surveillance requirements;
- separation of employee roles.

Exact cannabis security requirements must be sourced to the applicable regulator.

## 16. Corrective action

A compliance finding should distinguish:
- immediate containment;
- evidence preservation;
- root-cause investigation;
- correction of the specific record/product/process;
- systemic corrective action;
- effectiveness check;
- possible regulator notification when authoritative rules require it.

Do not destroy or rewrite evidence to make a record appear compliant.

## 17. Regulatory uncertainty

If the rule is ambiguous or sources conflict:
- cite both sources;
- state the ambiguity;
- avoid a definitive legal conclusion;
- recommend compliance counsel/regulator or authorized compliance leadership review.

## 18. Recommended answer pattern

For a compliance question:
1. Identify jurisdiction, facility/license context, date, and activity.
2. Retrieve authoritative current evidence.
3. Quote or paraphrase only the minimum necessary rule content.
4. Compare facility facts to the rule.
5. State compliant/noncompliant only when the evidence clearly supports it.
6. Identify missing evidence or ambiguity.
7. Separate operational best practice from legal requirement.
8. Recommend a human corrective/review action without mutating records.
9. Give confidence and source effective date/version.
