# Competitive homepage research

Reviewed 2026-09-11 (America/New_York). Primary sources are the nine current public homepages linked below. Initial review covered fetched content and information architecture; a subsequent live Chrome visual review of six benchmarks and the live DoobieLogic baseline is recorded below. This is not a measured motion, mobile, accessibility, performance, or structured-data audit of competitors. Exact authenticity of competitors' product imagery was not independently verified. Competitor claims are observations of their marketing, not independently verified facts or claims available to DoobieLogic.

## Recommended commercial structure

1. Name the category and outcome in the hero, with beta availability adjacent to the primary action.
2. Offer four operational paths: cultivation, production/manufacturing, retail and vertically integrated.
3. Show product evidence before a long explanation. Label every illustrative dataset and distinguish a real capture from a constructed preview.
4. Explain the handoff between departments and the shared facility context; avoid claiming every external system has been replaced.
5. State integration readiness in words, not vendor-logo implication.
6. Replace absent customer proof with bounded product evidence, visible beta status, traceability-validation transparency and controls described precisely.
7. Explain the evaluation process without promising a launch date, support response time or migration outcome.
8. Repeat the application action at natural decision points. Link resources only when they exist and are suitable for public visitors.

## Boundaries and remaining evidence

No competitor wording, customer logos, statistics, screenshots, testimonials or layouts should be copied into DoobieLogic. This review does not claim DoobieLogic is superior, faster, more secure or visually equivalent to any competitor. Those assertions require comparative evidence. Competitor FAQ markup, canonical tags and performance were not audited, so no technical SEO superiority is inferred from their navigation.

Customer proof remains a commercial gap that frontend design cannot close. The homepage should accept approved testimonials, case studies and quantified outcomes later, while rendering none until provenance and permission are present. Read `MARKETING_PROOF_LEDGER.md` for DoobieLogic-specific claim decisions.

## Actual visual comparison

Used the connected Chrome browser through `cua_repl` to visit and visually inspect rendered desktop viewport screenshots of the six core benchmarks below. These were current live pages, not search thumbnails. Browser viewport was approximately 1905 pixels wide; comparison with the new DoobieLogic artifact at 1440 pixels is directional, not pixel-matched. Initial Apex font loading and initial DoobieLogic loading states were allowed to resolve before judging. No forms were submitted and no customer material was copied into public assets.

| [Canix](https://www.canix.com/) | Navy hero, left outcome heading, prominent desktop UI with overlapping mobile UI at right; badges under CTA; cookie notice overlaps lower-left content. | Product evidence and outcome can coexist. New DoobieLogic uses a larger below-heading product frame; it does not show as much interface above the fold in the reviewed viewport. |
| [Distru](https://www.distru.com/) | Mint field, large bold centered category statement, compact rating above it, two distinct ERP/commerce conversion panels. | Strong type and intentional audience choice create hierarchy without a dense module wall. DoobieLogic keeps its own software-led direction and uses no borrowed scale proof. |

The live [DoobieLogic homepage](https://doobielogic.io/) was also viewed in Chrome. It had a tall list of buying, inventory, production, extraction, wholesale and compliance in its hero, a constructed operations dashboard to the right, dark/orange branding, and beta CTAs. The main and preview logo images appeared broken in this browser session; this observation does not establish a global outage. Live copy differed slightly from the starting local source, so the live baseline and recorded starting SHA should not be treated as identical deployments.

Compared with `artifacts/marketing-browser/chrome-1440-viewport.png`, the rebuilt local page has a shorter two-line headline, clearer category and beta labels, more whitespace, a visibly rendered local logo and a broad product-preview frame with workspace choices. It retains the brand identity instead of adopting a competitor palette. The first viewport shows the top of the product frame; full interface inspection requires scrolling or opening the full-size image. This is a design tradeoff, not proof of superior product visibility or conversion. The review supports category-appropriate hierarchy and a distinct identity; customer confidence, conversion lift and superiority require user research and actual outcomes.
